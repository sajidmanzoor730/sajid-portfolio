Operations · Pipeline Management

Lead Follow-up Pipeline

A structured pipeline framework for organizing lead stages, ownership, priorities, follow-up dates and next actions so outstanding work can be tracked consistently.

Project scope: This page demonstrates the structure and reporting logic of a lead follow-up workflow. It does not present fictional records or fabricated performance metrics as business results.

Pipeline structure

Lead identification

Capture the lead identifier, company or account information, source and initial contact details.

Stage management

Track the current stage consistently from new lead through qualification, contact, proposal and closure.

Ownership

Assign an owner so every active lead has a clear person responsible for the next action.

Follow-up control

Record the next follow-up date, required action and priority to reduce missed or delayed follow-ups.

Recommended data fields

Field Purpose
Lead_ID Unique identifier for each lead or account.
Company Company or account associated with the opportunity.
Lead_Source Source through which the lead entered the pipeline.
Stage Current position in the follow-up or sales process.
Owner Person responsible for managing the lead.
Priority Relative urgency used to organize follow-up work.
Last_Activity_Date Date of the most recent recorded activity.
Next_Follow_Up_Date Planned date for the next action.
Next_Action Specific action required to move the lead forward.
Status Current operational status of the lead.

Pipeline stages

New

Lead has been added but has not yet received an initial follow-up.

Contacted

Initial outreach has been completed and the next interaction is being tracked.

Qualified

The lead has met the defined qualification criteria and requires continued follow-up.

Proposal

A proposal or commercial discussion is active and requires a clearly defined next action.

Won

The opportunity has reached a successful outcome.

Closed / Lost

The opportunity is no longer active and should include an appropriate closure reason where available.

Operational checks

  • Every active lead should have an assigned owner.
  • Active leads should have a current pipeline stage.
  • Follow-up dates should be reviewed for overdue actions.
  • Next actions should be specific enough to be actionable.
  • Closed leads should have an appropriate outcome or closure reason.
  • Duplicate Lead_ID values should be identified before reporting.
  • Missing ownership and follow-up dates should be flagged for review.

Useful pipeline reporting

Stage distribution

Monitor how records are distributed across pipeline stages and identify where work is concentrated.

Follow-ups due

Identify upcoming and overdue follow-ups so pending actions can be prioritized.

Owner workload

Compare active pipeline workload across owners and identify unassigned records.

Pipeline aging

Track how long active leads remain in each stage to identify potential bottlenecks.

Technical Sourcing · Operations Analytics

Candidate Sourcing & Pipeline Analytics

A structured analytics framework for technical staffing workflows, connecting client requirements, candidate stages, aging, ownership, follow-ups, SLA/TAT and fulfillment reporting.

Why this project: My staffing operations experience involved active client requirements, candidate records, weekly staffing requests and pipeline tracking. This project translates that workflow into an analyst-friendly reporting model without presenting simulated records as workplace results.

What the analysis tracks

RequirementsOpen demand
Candidate stagePipeline flow
Requirement agingTime in stage
FulfillmentOutcome view

Core analytical views

Requirement pipeline

Track open roles by client, priority, owner, status and age so active demand is visible in one place.

Candidate funnel

Follow candidates through sourced, screened, submitted, interviewed, selected and closed stages.

SLA & TAT

Compare request dates, target dates and completion dates to identify delayed requirements and recurring bottlenecks.

Fulfillment analysis

Connect candidate movement to requirement outcomes and identify where the pipeline loses momentum.

Data model

  • Requirement: requirement ID, client, role, priority, owner, opened date, target date and status.
  • Candidate: candidate ID, source, stage, submission date, interview status and outcome.
  • Activity: follow-up date, action, owner, activity type and timestamp.
  • Metrics: aging, SLA/TAT, stage conversion, fulfillment and workload.

Workplace relevance

This project reflects the same analytical questions that matter in staffing operations: What is open? Where is each requirement in the pipeline? Which items are aging? Who owns the next action? Where are SLA or fulfillment delays appearing?

It demonstrates how operational staffing data can be structured for repeatable reporting and decision support rather than treated as a simple candidate list.

Operations SOP & Runbook

A structured operational playbook covering onboarding, access reviews, recurring checks, escalation and handoffs with clear ownership and evidence requirements.

Project scope: This page demonstrates SOP and runbook design principles. It does not contain real employee credentials, customer information, internal company procedures or confidential operational records.

Core operational procedures

01 · Onboarding

Confirm the request owner, required access, documentation and onboarding checklist before the process begins.

Owner Access Checklist

02 · Access Review

Verify required systems and remove unnecessary access through the appropriate approval and request process.

Security Audit Approval

03 · Recurring Checks

Review queues, overdue tasks, failed processes, data-quality exceptions and SLA-related issues on a defined schedule.

Daily SLA Quality

04 · Escalation

Capture impact, evidence, owner, next action and escalation timing before handing an issue to the appropriate team.

Incident Evidence Handoff

Standard runbook structure

Step Required action Owner Evidence
1 Receive and review the operational request. Assigned owner Request or ticket reference
2 Confirm scope, priority and required approvals. Process owner Approval or request details
3 Complete the required operational checks. Assigned operator Checklist or system record
4 Document the outcome and any exceptions. Assigned operator Notes or supporting evidence
5 Escalate unresolved issues when defined thresholds are reached. Process owner Escalation record
6 Complete the handoff and confirm next ownership. Outgoing owner Handoff note

Daily operations checklist

  • Review open and overdue operational work.
  • Check SLA exceptions and aging items.
  • Review failed or incomplete automated processes.
  • Check for data-quality issues requiring correction.
  • Confirm ownership for unresolved items.
  • Document important exceptions and follow-up actions.

Escalation requirements

Impact

Clearly describe what is affected and the operational consequence.

Evidence

Include relevant records, timestamps, screenshots or system references where appropriate.

Owner

Identify the current owner and the team responsible for the next action.

Next action

State what needs to happen next and whether a deadline or SLA applies.

Handoff checklist

  • Current status is documented.
  • Outstanding actions are clearly listed.
  • Relevant evidence is attached or referenced.
  • Next owner is identified.
  • Priority and SLA expectations are clear.
  • Follow-up date is recorded when required.
