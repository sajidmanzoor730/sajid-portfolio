Investigation objective

Investigation workflow

The analysis follows a practical sequence from alert intake to measurable operational outcome.

01

Alert intake

Review alert name, severity, source, affected device/entity and available evidence before deciding investigation priority.

02

Evidence review

Correlate alert context with related entities and activity to understand what happened and whether additional investigation is required.

03

KQL analysis

Use filtering, projection, aggregation and entity-level queries to isolate relevant security activity and identify patterns.

04

Outcome & KPI

Record investigation status and response action, then analyze severity, resolution rate and MTTR for operational reporting.

Detailed investigation cases

Three representative investigations show how the project connects alert context, evidence review, technique mapping and outcome.

Case 01 · High

Suspicious PowerShell activity

Technique: T1059.001 — PowerShell.

Focus: Review process context, affected endpoint and account activity, then determine whether the execution pattern required escalation.

OutcomeInvestigation recorded as resolved after endpoint evidence review and response validation.
Case 02 · High

Credential-access activity

Technique: T1003 — OS Credential Dumping.

Focus: Correlate account and process evidence, assess the affected entity and prioritize the alert based on severity.

OutcomeInvestigation recorded as resolved with credential-protection and follow-up review as the response focus.
Case 03 · Medium

Unusual network activity

Technique: T1071 — Application Layer Protocol.

Focus: Review connection context and destination information, then determine whether additional telemetry was needed.

OutcomeInvestigation remained open for continued review rather than being marked resolved prematurely.

KQL investigation

Queries were used to filter, correlate, summarize and prioritize security events before interpreting the results.

Severity prioritization

AlertInfo
| where Timestamp > ago(30d)
| where Severity == "High"
| project Timestamp, AlertId, Title, Severity, ServiceSource, DetectionSource, AttackTechniques
| order by Timestamp desc
Why it mattersCreates a focused queue for high-severity investigation instead of scanning every event equally.

Alert + evidence correlation

AlertInfo
| where Timestamp > ago(30d)
| join kind=leftouter (
    AlertEvidence
    | project AlertId, DeviceName, AccountName, FileName, EntityType, RemoteIP
) on AlertId
| project Timestamp, AlertId, Title, Severity, DeviceName,
          AccountName, FileName, EntityType, RemoteIP
| order by Timestamp desc
Why it mattersConnects the alert record with relevant evidence so the analyst can move from an alert title to affected entities and activity context.

Repeated entity analysis

AlertEvidence
| where Timestamp > ago(30d)
| where isnotempty(DeviceName)
| summarize AlertCount = dcount(AlertId) by DeviceName
| order by AlertCount desc
Why it mattersSurfaces endpoints associated with repeated alerts and helps prioritize entities for deeper review.

Severity distribution

AlertInfo
| where Timestamp > ago(30d)
| summarize AlertCount = count() by Severity
| order by AlertCount desc
Why it mattersConverts event volume into a management-friendly severity distribution for security operations reporting.

Detection-source analysis

AlertInfo
| where Timestamp > ago(30d)
| summarize AlertCount = count() by DetectionSource
| order by AlertCount desc
Why it mattersShows which detection sources contribute most to the investigation workload and supports operational trend analysis.

Operational findings

Severity required prioritization.

Half of the investigated alerts were high severity, making severity-based triage an important first filter for investigation workload.

Resolution became measurable.

10 of 12 investigations were resolved, while two remained under investigation rather than being forced into a closed state.

MTTR connected technical work to operations.

The average resolution time across resolved investigations was 25.3 minutes, providing a practical service-operations KPI.

Evidence structure supports repeatability.

Capturing category, source, technique, investigation action and outcome creates a consistent record for later trend analysis.
